**Status: unvalidated with real users as of 2026-11-09.** No studio owner, freelancer, project manager or payment administrator was interviewed or observed in this session. Meera, the six-person studio and the three-hour weekly loss remain illustrative; they are not evidence.

## Research limitation

This environment provides no participant access, recruiting channel, observed customer workflow or production usage data. No interviews, quotations, baseline timings, corrections or willingness-to-delegate results have been collected. The scenario fixtures demonstrate software behavior, not market demand or operational time savings.

## Required field study

Recruit 5–8 participants across Indian small-studio owners, creative-agency payment administrators, project managers and freelancers, with variation in entity type and contractor volume. Obtain informed consent and anonymize notes. Do not use hackathon teammates as substitute customers.

For each participant:

1. Observe one real payment-allocation run using existing tools, with sensitive values redacted.
2. Record start/end time, systems opened, manual steps, corrections, approval delays, exceptions and number of interventions.
3. Map who decides contractual amount, priority, recipient, timing and execution authority.
4. Ask about delayed client receipts, competing deadlines, partial schedules, failed transfers and disputes.
5. Test whether contextual scenario comparison addresses an observed bottleneck rather than leading with “AI.”
6. Measure understanding, challenge/rejection behavior and conditions under which delegation is acceptable.
7. Record disconfirming cases where deterministic due-date ordering is already sufficient.
